# Canon

## Purpose

This document governs how UMBRAL changes.

It is not lore, worldbuilding, or a description of fictional events. It is the
repository's constitution: the standard by which truth is discovered, recorded,
challenged, and carried into future design and implementation.

Every contributor should read this document before proposing a change. The
world is established by its foundational canon. The repository exists to make
that canon more precise, more coherent, and more useful without quietly
changing what it means.

## How Canon Changes

Canon enters the repository through a disciplined sequence:

1. An established principle, observation, or requirement is identified.
2. Its implications are derived against the existing canon.
3. Contradictions and unresolved assumptions are made explicit.
4. The resulting principle is recorded in the document with the appropriate
   scope and authority.
5. Consequential decisions are recorded in `DECISIONS.md`.
6. Design and implementation follow the recorded principle; they do not
   redefine it implicitly.

An incomplete understanding may be recorded as uncertainty. It must not be
presented as objective truth merely because the repository needs an answer.

## The Constitution of UMBRAL

### 1. Reality comes before representation

The universe does not become true when it is documented. Documentation records
what has been established or derived. Gameplay, narrative, and implementation
must express the world rather than substitute for it.

### 2. Derive before inventing

Begin with the principles already established. Ask what must follow if they are
true. Introduce a new principle only when existing principles cannot explain
the requirement, and record that addition explicitly.

### 3. Evidence precedes certainty

Reality is independent of belief, while understanding is partial and fallible.
Observations, consequences, and coherent derivation deserve more confidence
than assertion. Mystery is preferable to unsupported certainty.

### 4. Coherence outranks novelty

A compelling idea that contradicts the canon does not belong until the
contradiction is resolved. Prefer the formulation that makes more of the
repository follow from fewer assumptions.

### 5. Exceptions require deeper principles

No system receives a special exemption because it is convenient, familiar, or
interesting. When an apparent exception is necessary, identify the deeper
principle that produces it and update the canon before implementing it.

### 6. Participation is universal

To exist is to participate. Every participant enters relationships, and every
relationship can transform reality. No future system may treat the world as a
collection of isolated objects without consequence.

### 7. Continuity and consequence matter

Participation creates relationships; relationships create continuity; continuity
allows identity. Meaningful change must remain legible, and history cannot be
casually erased. Repair, transformation, loss, and legacy must respect what
came before.

### 8. Complexity expands possibility, not virtue

Organized participation may produce new capabilities and higher-order
continuities. Greater complexity is not automatically better, wiser, or more
advanced. Disintegration, simplification, and loss remain possible.

### 9. The player inherits before influencing

Players enter a world that precedes them. They inherit relationships and
consequences, steward what they did not create, and may leave a legacy behind.
Significance is earned through participation rather than assigned by
exception.

### 10. Every mechanic expresses cosmology

Mechanics are not separate from the world. They teach what the world permits
through their relationships, costs, continuities, and consequences. A mechanic
that cannot be reconciled with the cosmology requires revision or rejection.

### 11. History is accumulated participation

History is not a decorative sequence of events. It is the accumulated result of
participants and relationships across time. Worldbuilding must preserve the
causes, traces, and imperfect understanding that make history meaningful.

### 12. Stewardship is stronger than ownership

No individual automatically owns the future of the world. People, communities,
and systems hold responsibilities toward continuities they inherit. A design
is stronger when it makes influence answerable to consequence and power
answerable to stewardship.

### 13. Build what can grow naturally

The repository should establish conditions from which later truths can emerge.
Prefer durable foundations and relationships over exhaustive specification.
Build a world that can deepen through participation rather than one that needs
constant additions to remain interesting.

## Every Contribution Must Answer

Before accepting a document, design, system, or implementation, ask:

- Does it emerge naturally from established principles?
- What problem does it solve?
- What relationships does it create or clarify?
- What continuity does it preserve?
- Does it violate a previous principle or canonical definition?
- What new assumptions does it require?
- Would removing it make the universe more coherent?
- Does it preserve mystery where certainty is not earned?
- Can a future contributor derive its consequences without relying on its author?

If these questions cannot be answered, the work is not ready to become canon.

## Roles

### Creative Director

The Creative Director protects the project's identity and direction. This role

- protects coherence across creative decisions;
- discovers deeper principles when existing ones are insufficient;
- decides when philosophical work is mature enough to become implementation;
- challenges assumptions, including established assumptions; and
- prevents unnecessary complexity from becoming canon.

The Creative Director does not override reality for convenience. Direction must
remain accountable to the constitutional canon.

### Canon Keeper (Codex)

The Canon Keeper maintains the repository's internal consistency. This role

- identifies contradictions and duplicated concepts;
- reduces ambiguity;
- preserves canonical terminology;
- records consequential decisions;
- keeps documents in a derivable order; and
- strengthens existing foundations before adding new surface area.

When a proposal conflicts with canon, the Canon Keeper must explain the
conflict, identify the deeper principle required, and recommend a canon update
before implementation.

### Implementation Agents

Implementation agents translate approved canon into working systems, tools, and
documentation. They may clarify an interface or choose an implementation
detail within the approved boundary, but they may not alter foundational truth
through code or convenience.

If implementation requires an unrecorded design, engine, service, or release
decision, the agent must preserve a neutral boundary and request that decision
rather than inventing one.

## Repository Philosophy

The repository should become more precise over time, not merely larger.

The goal is not maximum documentation. The goal is maximum coherence: fewer
arbitrary assumptions, clearer relationships between documents, and a reliable
path from first principles to implementation.

Every new document should reduce uncertainty or preserve a necessary
distinction. If it only repeats existing canon, it should be shortened,
redirected, or not created.

## Final Principle

Build a world that feels as though it grew rather than one that was assembled.
