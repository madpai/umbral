# Participation

## Purpose

Cosmology establishes that reality is participatory. This document defines the
process by which that participation produces continuity, identity, autonomy,
reflection, and collective order.

Existence alone does not explain reality. Existence is the condition from which
reality begins; participation is how reality unfolds. Every participant enters
relationships, and those relationships create the conditions from which more
complex forms of continuity may emerge.

This document is a bridge between [Cosmology](COSMOLOGY.md) and the future
account of `LIFE.md`. It defines no species, culture, or mechanic.

## To Exist Is To Participate

To exist is to participate. Every participant inevitably affects reality simply
by existing, because existence occurs within relationships rather than outside
them.

Participation does not require movement, choice, awareness, or intention. A
participant may alter conditions around it, be altered by those conditions, or
persist as part of a relationship whose effects continue through time. No
participant can be completely inert with respect to reality.

Participation is therefore unavoidable. The question is not whether something
participates, but how it participates and what continuity its relationships
make possible.

## Participants

A participant is any enduring entity whose existence necessarily enters into
relationships with other aspects of reality.

“Enduring” does not mean permanent. It means that an entity has sufficient
continuity to be distinguished across some interval of change and to enter
relationships within that interval. Particles, mountains, rivers, stars,
organisms, and civilizations are all participants. They differ in scale,
duration, organization, and capacity, but none is an exception to
participation.

A participant is not defined by size, material, complexity, or consciousness.
Those properties affect how it participates. They do not determine whether it
participates.

## Participation

Participation is the continual formation, transformation, and dissolution of
relationships within reality.

Participation is not action. Action is one expression of participation by an
autonomous participant. Participation is not intention. Intention is one
expression of participation by a reflective participant.

Passive objects participate because their existence enters relationships. A
rock participates in pressure, weather, terrain, and the organisms that depend
upon it. Water participates in flow, erosion, climate, and every continuity
that depends upon its presence. Their lack of intention does not make their
relationships unreal.

Participation changes both the participants and the conditions in which they
exist. Through time, repeated relationships may become organized continuities
with properties not present in any isolated interaction.

## Relationships

Participation creates relationships. Relationships create continuity.
Continuity allows identity. Identity allows organized continuities to persist,
interact, and become capable of new forms.

A relationship may be physical, spatial, temporal, material, ecological,
social, linguistic, or historical. Its kind does not determine its importance.
Its persistence and consequences determine what it makes possible.

Continuity does not prevent change. It is what allows change to occur without
requiring replacement. When relationships remain sufficiently organized across
change, a participant retains identity. When those continuities interact,
higher-order identities and capabilities may emerge.

Persistent meaningful relationships may acquire Resonance. Resonance is the
universal behavior through which significant continuity persists in reality;
its laws are defined in [Resonance](RESONANCE.md).

## Passive Participation

A passive participant participates without sustaining or directing its own
continuity through internally organized processes.

Rocks, water, mountains, planets, and stars are passive participants. They
affect and are affected by other participants through structure, position,
motion, material properties, and persistence. Their participation may be
immense in consequence without being autonomous.

Passive participation is not lesser participation. It describes a mode of
participation, not a measure of value, significance, or influence.

## Autonomous Participation

Autonomous participation is the capacity of a participant to sustain and direct
its own continuity through internally organized processes.

An autonomous participant can preserve its organization while conditions
change. It may grow, repair damage, regulate internal conditions, and adapt its
organization in response to relationships with its surroundings. These
capacities need not be conscious or intentional. They follow from organization
that maintains itself rather than merely persisting through external forces.

Autonomy is a capacity, not complete independence. Every autonomous
participant remains dependent upon relationships with other participants and
with the conditions that sustain it. Its continuity is self-maintained, never
self-created or isolated.

Life is the particular pattern of continuity that actively maintains and
reproduces itself through participation. The full consequences of autonomous
participation are developed in `LIFE.md`.

## Reflective Participation

Reflective participation occurs when an autonomous participant can model both
reality and its own participation within reality.

Reflection makes it possible to represent conditions that are not immediately
present. From this capacity follow planning, abstraction, purpose, teaching,
and wisdom as possible forms of participation. Reflection does not remove a
participant from causality. It gives the participant additional ways to model
relationships and guide its continuity through imagined possibilities.

Reflective participation prepares the distinction between participation that
is merely sustained and participation that is experienced, interpreted, and
deliberately directed. Consciousness is defined from this foundation in a
future canonical document.

## Collective Participation

When multiple autonomous participants sustain relationships over time, those
relationships may form a higher-order continuity. Families, guilds, villages,
cities, and civilizations are examples of collective participants.

A collective participant has continuity and identity when its relationships,
roles, practices, structures, and memory persist through changes in its
individual members. Its identity is not reducible to one member, even though
it depends upon its members and their participation.

A collective participant is not automatically a conscious organism. Collective
continuity can organize behavior, preserve knowledge, and direct shared work
without producing a single unified subjective experience.

## Cooperation

Cooperation emerges when multiple participants preserve greater continuity
together than they could preserve separately under the same conditions.

Cooperation is therefore a structural consequence of participation, not a
moral category. It may preserve a family, a settlement, a practice, a route,
or any other continuity that depends upon coordinated relationships. Whether a
particular cooperation is beneficial or harmful is determined by its
consequences, not by the existence of cooperation itself.

## Competition

Competition emerges when the continuities of participants cannot be
simultaneously preserved under existing constraints.

Those constraints may concern space, time, material, access, knowledge, or
other relationships. Competition does not require moral opposition. It is the
condition produced when participants pursue continuities whose requirements
overlap in ways that prevent them from all continuing unchanged.

Competition may produce conflict, separation, adaptation, or new cooperation.
Its outcome remains open because Resonance influences possibility without
completely determining the future.

## Emergence

Emergence is the appearance of new capabilities that cannot be reduced to the
behavior of individual parts while remaining dependent upon those parts.

Emergence is recursive. Relationships among particles may form structures with
new properties. Structures may form organized processes. Organized processes
may form organisms. Organisms may form ecosystems. People may form
civilizations. At every level, the higher-order continuity depends upon its
participants while possessing relationships and capabilities that no isolated
participant possesses.

Emergence does not introduce a separate substance or exception to reality. It
is what organized participation looks like at a higher level of continuity.
It does not guarantee improvement, harmony, or increasing complexity in every
case. Disintegration remains possible at every level.

## Disintegration

Disintegration is the loss of organized continuity.

Disintegration is not death and is not evil. It may be partial, temporary, or
reversible. A participant may lose an organization while retaining another;
relationships may fragment and later reorganize; a collective may cease to
function while its members continue.

Death is a specific irreversible end of autonomous participation. Disintegration
may contribute to death, but it may also produce repair, transformation, or the
emergence of a different continuity. In every case, the relationships and
consequences that preceded disintegration remain part of reality.

## Implications

Reality becomes capable of increasing complexity because participation
continually creates relationships, and relationships create opportunities for
new forms of organized continuity. Complexity is not a promise of progress or
a predetermined direction. It is an available consequence of persistence,
interaction, and emergence.

Every participant contributes to the conditions from which future participants
may arise. Passive participation provides conditions. Autonomous participation
sustains and directs continuity. Reflective participation models possibility.
Collective participation preserves patterns beyond individual lifetimes.

This is the foundation from which `LIFE.md`, consciousness, emergence, and all
later accounts of living systems must follow.
