# The First Valley Initiative

## Purpose

The First Valley is a stress test of the UMBRAL canon, not merely a starting
region. Its purpose is to determine whether reality, participation, continuity,
and consequence can generate a coherent, believable place without arbitrary
invention.

This document is a controlled worldbuilding derivation and validation record.
It preserves durable direction while distinguishing what has been established
from what remains hypothetical, evocative, or unresolved. It is not finished
lore. No provisional statement becomes canon through repetition.

The initiative must remain compatible with [CANON](../../CANON.md),
[Cosmology](COSMOLOGY.md), and [Participation](PARTICIPATION.md).

## Status Vocabulary

- **Established decisions** derive from approved canon or an explicitly
  recorded project decision. They constrain later work.
- **Working hypotheses** are provisional models used for testing. They may be
  revised or rejected when physical, ecological, or historical constraints
  require it.
- **Evocative possibilities** are prompts for investigation. They are not
  commitments and must not be used as premises for further derivation.
- **Unresolved questions** identify missing constraints or knowledge. They must
  remain open until evidence or derivation provides a sufficient answer.

## Success Criterion

A player should enter one valley and feel that everything belongs there and
would continue existing without them.

The valley succeeds when its geography, ecology, settlements, relationships,
and history appear to have grown from shared conditions. It must not feel like
a collection of attractions arranged around the player. The player enters a
place already in motion, inherits responsibilities that predate them, and may
alter continuities that will persist after they leave.

## Established Direction

The following constraints are established for the initiative:

- Derive before inventing.
- Begin with reality before gameplay.
- Do not use feature-first worldbuilding to justify a place.
- Every element must answer why it exists.
- The world does not exist for the player.
- The player enters a place already in motion.
- Settlements are communities with memory, not service locations.
- History is accumulated participation.
- Stewardship is more important than ownership.
- Caretakers are more foundational than heroes.

A place becomes real when it remembers.

The world already had responsibilities before the player arrived. The First
Valley must make those responsibilities visible through relationships,
structures, work, and accumulated change rather than exposition alone.

## Derivation Method

The valley must be derived in this order:

1. **Reality** — establish the physical conditions permitted by cosmology and
   participation.
2. **Ecology** — derive flows, dependencies, cycles, and limits from those
   physical conditions.
3. **Intelligent participation** — determine how reflective participants could
   perceive, use, alter, and depend upon the existing conditions.
4. **Settlement** — derive durable habitation from movement, shelter, resources,
   risk, and relationships.
5. **Civilization** — derive institutions, practices, and collective
   continuities from settlement needs and accumulated participation.
6. **History** — record the consequences of those continuities through time.
7. **Present conditions** — identify what remains active, damaged, contested,
   forgotten, or under pressure.
8. **Player participation** — define where a player can inherit, steward, alter,
   or leave something within the existing continuities.

Later stages must not be used to justify earlier ones. A desired settlement
cannot determine the river. A desired profession cannot determine the valley's
resources. A desired player activity cannot determine what the place needs.

## Validation Questions

Every proposed element must be tested against the following questions:

- Why is this here?
- What problem does it solve?
- What relationships sustain it?
- What continuity does it preserve?
- What consequences did it create?
- What would change if it disappeared?
- Would the earlier layers still make sense without intelligent life?
- Does this follow from the layers before it?
- Does it feel grown or assembled?
- Would the place continue existing without the player?

An element that cannot answer these questions is not ready to be established.

## Current Geographic Hypothesis

The following is a **working hypothesis**, not finalized geography:

- A valley is enclosed on three sides by old, weathered mountains.
- A river enters from northern highlands.
- The river leaves through a narrow eastern gorge.
- Western slopes are wetter and support denser forest.
- Eastern slopes are drier and transition toward grassland.
- Workable stone is available in the surrounding hills.
- A natural crossing or ford concentrates travel and exchange.

These conditions are useful because they create possible relationships among
water, elevation, forest, grassland, stone, movement, shelter, and risk. They do
not yet establish a geology, climate, seasonal cycle, soil system, or hazard
model. Those physical constraints are the next gate.

No final proper names should be assigned at this stage.

## Current Settlement Hypothesis

The following is a **working hypothesis** about how settlement could emerge
from the geographic conditions:

- The first settlement forms at a crossing.
- Repeated travel creates the first road.
- The earliest permanent structure begins as shelter rather than architecture.
- Permanence is confirmed when additional families choose to stay.
- The first communal agreement concerns mutual survival and rebuilding.
- Early conflict comes from competing responsibilities rather than simple evil.
- The first generation born there experiences the valley as home rather than
  frontier.

This chain must remain subordinate to the physical and ecological derivation.
The crossing, road, shelter, families, agreement, conflict, and sense of home
must each acquire their place through demonstrated relationships and
consequences.

## Community Memory

The following are **established design rules** for the initiative:

- Every settlement must have a real founding chain.
- Every road began as repeated movement.
- Every ruin once solved a practical problem.
- Every tradition must have an origin.
- Meaningful structures accumulate repairs, alterations, and remembered events.
- Memory is a communal resource, preserved imperfectly through participants.
- Locations should display historical layers rather than static lore labels.

These rules make history accumulated participation rather than decoration. A
structure should show what it has endured. A path should reveal why movement
repeated. A tradition should retain evidence of the problem that made it
necessary, even when its original meaning has become incomplete.

## Caretakers

The early memorable inhabitants should primarily be people who maintain
continuity. Possible roles include a bridgekeeper, ferryperson, miller, mason,
forester, or herbalist.

These are **established design directions**, not final characters. No final
names, biographies, species, or personal histories should be created here.

The opening experience should foreground responsibility and stewardship rather
than heroic exceptionalism. Caretakers reveal the valley by showing what must be
maintained for other continuities to persist. Their importance comes from the
relationships they sustain, not from being uniquely chosen.

## Working Possibilities

The following are **evocative possibilities** subject to validation:

- The first major ruin may be an abandoned quarry.
- The crossing may later become a bridge.
- Bridge maintenance may become an early player responsibility.
- The first memorable profession may be a bridgekeeper or crossing steward.
- An old meeting tree may predate formal civic institutions.

These possibilities may be retained only if the physical, ecological, and
historical derivation gives them a necessary place. They must not be treated as
canon, promises, quests, or required features.

## Unresolved Questions

The following questions remain open:

- Why is this specific valley the first playable place?
- What geological process formed it?
- What climate and seasonal cycle govern it?
- What species belong here?
- Who were the earliest settlers?
- What nearly caused the settlement to fail?
- What historical layers remain visible?
- What present tension invites player participation?
- Which ideas require `LIFE.md` or a future `ECOLOGY.md` before they can be
  canonized?

No settlement history should be expanded to answer these questions by
assumption. Missing physical constraints are a reason to pause, not an
invitation to decorate.
