# UMBRAL Game Design Document

> **Status:** Living Design Document
> **Purpose:** Define the guiding principles, gameplay philosophy, and core systems of UMBRAL. This document intentionally describes *what* the game should become, not *how* individual systems will be implemented.

---

# 1. Vision

UMBRAL is a persistent online world designed to feel inhabited rather than consumed.

The game exists to create a world where players develop a lasting relationship
with the places they explore, the people they meet, and the history they help
create.

Every gameplay system should strengthen the feeling that the player is living
inside a coherent world rather than progressing through disconnected content.

Whenever a design decision conflicts with this philosophy, the world should
take priority over convenience.

---

# 2. Design Pillars

## A Living World

The world exists independently of the player.

Players discover it rather than create it from nothing.

---

## Meaningful Persistence

Time matters.

Player actions should have consequences appropriate to their scale.

Persistence is not unlimited permanence—it is meaningful continuity.

---

## Player Agency

Players should have freedom to pursue their own goals while respecting the
integrity of the shared world.

Agency comes from meaningful choices rather than unlimited options.

---

## Social Worlds

Other players should feel like fellow inhabitants, not background NPCs with
human usernames.

Systems should encourage cooperation, rivalry, reputation, and community.

---

## Discovery

Knowledge should be valuable.

Exploration should reward curiosity, observation, experimentation, and
learning—not simply map completion.

---

# 3. Core Gameplay Loop

The intended gameplay loop is built around participation in the world rather
than repetitive task completion.

At a high level:

Explore

?

Learn

?

Participate

?

Influence

?

Grow

?

Return

Each cycle should deepen the player's understanding of the world while opening
new possibilities for future play.

Exact mechanics remain design work.

---

# 4. World Structure

The world should feel ancient, interconnected, and internally consistent.

Major regions should possess distinct identities without becoming isolated
games of their own.

History should influence geography.

Culture should influence behavior.

The environment should tell stories without requiring explicit narration.

---

# 5. Player Identity

Players are inhabitants of UMBRAL.

They are not automatically heroes.

Their identity is earned through their actions, relationships, and reputation.

The world should recognize what players choose to become rather than assigning
them predefined importance.

---

# 6. Progression

Progression should reflect increasing understanding and capability rather than
simply increasing numerical power.

Players should grow through:

* knowledge
* experience
* relationships
* mastery
* responsibility

Numbers may support progression, but they should never define it.

---

# 7. Conflict

Conflict should exist because the world creates meaningful reasons for it.

Combat is one expression of conflict—not the purpose of the game.

Diplomacy, trade, exploration, discovery, competition, cooperation, and
politics are equally valid forms of interaction.

Violence should carry context and consequences.

---

# 8. Economy

The economy should emerge naturally from the world rather than existing as an
isolated trading interface.

Goods should have purpose.

Resources should have context.

Production should matter because the world requires it.

The economy should strengthen player interdependence.

---

# 9. Community

Communities should form naturally through shared goals, geography,
relationships, and history.

The game should encourage players to build identities larger than themselves.

Long-term social bonds are more valuable than short-term matchmaking.

---

# 10. Technical Philosophy

Technology exists to support immersion.

Implementation choices should preserve the long-term vision of the world.

Scalability should never come at the expense of coherence.

Architecture decisions belong in the Architecture documentation.

---

# 11. Design Constraints

Every new feature should answer:

* Does this make the world feel more alive?
* Does this strengthen player agency?
* Does this preserve world coherence?
* Does this create meaningful decisions?
* Will this still make sense five years from now?

If the answer is "no," the feature should be reconsidered.

---

# 12. Non-Goals

UMBRAL is not attempting to become:

* a checklist-driven content treadmill
* a collection of disconnected mechanics
* a race toward maximum level
* a theme park built around temporary engagement
* a world that exists only while the player is looking at it

The project prioritizes depth, continuity, and identity over feature count.

---

# 13. Open Design Questions

The following decisions intentionally remain unresolved:

* Character progression model
* Skill systems
* Combat implementation
* Death philosophy
* Crafting systems
* Economy implementation
* World simulation depth
* Settlement ownership
* Governance systems
* Narrative delivery

These topics will each receive dedicated design documents and Architecture
Decision Records (ADRs) before implementation begins.
