# The First Hour

## Purpose

This is a paper vertical slice intended to prove that UMBRAL is engaging as a
game before a larger implementation effort begins.

This document is a framework and controlled design record. It does not finalize
dialogue, NPC biographies, a prophecy, an amnesia premise, a chosen-one role, a
cinematic opening, or a quest chain.

The following distinctions apply:

- **Canonical requirement:** a constraint that follows from approved project
  direction or this framework.
- **Hypothesis:** a provisional proposal to test.
- **Example:** an illustrative possibility that is not a commitment.
- **Unresolved decision:** an open question requiring evidence or approval.

No beat in this document is finalized content.

## Success Criterion

A player who skips all dialogue should still:

- understand how to move and interact;
- discover something interesting;
- perform satisfying actions;
- encounter a meaningful decision or consequence;
- experience danger;
- receive a rewarding result;
- identify at least one long-term personal goal; and
- feel that the world existed before their arrival.

## Player-Experience Principles

- The player is a participant, not the center of reality.
- Teaching should occur through action wherever possible.
- Exposition must not block play.
- The opening should introduce stewardship without becoming unpaid chore
  simulation.
- Every activity must contain tension, mastery, discovery, choice, or reward.
- Responsibilities must be mechanically engaging.
- The first hour should reveal several playstyles rather than forcing one
  identity.
- Combat should not be delayed merely to make a philosophical statement.
- Combat should appear when it becomes naturally and mechanically useful.
- The opening must respect player agency.

## Engagement Layers

Players may engage with any or all of these layers:

1. **Immediate Fun** — the direct pleasure of responsive, readable, satisfying
   actions.
2. **Living Systems** — the discovery of relationships, routines,
   consequences, and continuity through play.
3. **Meaning** — the themes and significance that emerge through participation,
   choices, and what persists.

No layer is a prerequisite for enjoying the others. Authored narrative and
dialogue are optional engagement layers rather than the sole means of reaching
the living world or its meaning.

## Player Archetypes to Support

The framework must avoid communicating that only one playstyle is legitimate.
It should support, at minimum:

- explorer;
- fighter;
- gatherer;
- crafter;
- trader;
- social or cooperative player;
- narrative-focused player; and
- efficiency-focused player.

Every archetype does not require equal depth within the first hour. Each must
remain a valid direction for later participation.

## Anti-Patterns

- Dialogue before meaningful control.
- Tutorial chores without mastery or choice.
- Mandatory lore comprehension.
- Fake choices.
- Excessive walking without discovery.
- Delayed access to enjoyable mechanics.
- Opening exposition dumps.
- Treating ordinary work as inherently fun.
- Assuming realism automatically creates engagement.
- Using philosophy to defend weak gameplay.

## Validation Questions

- What does the player physically do?
- Why is that action enjoyable?
- What skill can the player improve?
- What choice is being made?
- What changes because of the action?
- What feedback confirms success or failure?
- Would this remain engaging with all dialogue skipped?
- Does it support more than one player motivation?
- Does it belong in the First Valley?
- Can it be prototyped cheaply?
- What evidence would cause us to remove it?

## Open Decisions

- How the player enters the valley.
- The first controllable action.
- The first repeatable activity.
- When danger appears.
- When combat appears.
- How gathering and crafting are introduced.
- How player preference first becomes visible.
- What creates the first strong reward.
- What long-term aspiration is revealed.
- What the smallest playable prototype must contain.

These remain unresolved decisions. No first-hour sequence is finalized by this
framework.

## Next Gate

Design the first-hour experience beat by beat, then select the smallest 10–15
minute segment capable of testing movement, interaction, feedback, one
meaningful activity, and one consequence.

No full hour should be implemented before that smaller segment proves
enjoyable.
